Title: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Internet Addiction (CBT-IA)
Source: Young (2013) - Treatment outcomes using CBT-IA
Category: coping_strategies

------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Internet Addiction (CBT-IA) is an effective treatment approach designed to reduce compulsive internet and social media use.

The therapy focuses on understanding the relationship between thoughts, emotions, and behaviors, and helps individuals regain control over their internet usage.

Studies show that:
- Over 95% of patients improved after 12 weeks of treatment
- 78% maintained recovery after 6 months :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

------------------------------------------------------------
CORE IDEAS
------------------------------------------------------------
Internet addiction is driven by:
- compulsive behavior patterns
- negative thought patterns
- emotional triggers (stress, loneliness, boredom)

CBT works by:
- increasing awareness of behavior
- changing harmful thinking patterns
- building healthier habits

------------------------------------------------------------
CBT-IA MODEL (3 PHASES)
------------------------------------------------------------

1. BEHAVIOR MODIFICATION
- Identify problematic internet use patterns
- Reduce time spent online
- Set limits and schedules
- Replace excessive usage with healthier activities

2. COGNITIVE RESTRUCTURING
- Identify negative thoughts driving usage
- Challenge irrational beliefs (e.g., "I need to check constantly")
- Replace with healthier thinking patterns

3. HARM REDUCTION THERAPY (HRT)
- Address underlying psychological issues (stress, anxiety, depression)
- Improve relationships and social interactions
- Prevent relapse by building long-term coping strategies :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

------------------------------------------------------------
PRACTICAL STRATEGIES
------------------------------------------------------------

- Track daily internet/social media usage
- Set fixed time limits for usage
- Avoid high-risk triggers (late night use, boredom)
- Create alternative activities (exercise, hobbies, social interaction)
- Use journaling to reflect on usage patterns
- Practice mindfulness and awareness of urges
- Improve time management habits
- Build offline routines (especially before sleep)

------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGERS IDENTIFIED
------------------------------------------------------------

Common triggers for excessive internet use:
- boredom
- stress
- emotional discomfort
- social isolation
- avoidance of responsibilities

------------------------------------------------------------
RECOVERY INSIGHTS
------------------------------------------------------------

- Recovery is a gradual process
- Relapse can occur and should be expected
- Long-term success depends on:
  - behavior awareness
  - consistent habit change
  - emotional regulation

------------------------------------------------------------
IMPORTANT NOTES FOR AI SYSTEM
------------------------------------------------------------

- Do NOT diagnose users
- Provide supportive and non-judgmental suggestions
- Focus on small, practical behavior changes
- Encourage awareness and self-reflection
- Suggest gradual reduction, not immediate elimination
