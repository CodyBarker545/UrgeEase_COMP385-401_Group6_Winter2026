Title: Treating Internet Addiction with Cognitive Behavioral Therapy: Lifestyle Training Approach

Source:
Van Rooij, A. J., Zinn, M. F., Schoenmakers, T. M., & Van de Mheen, D. (2012)

Summary:
This study evaluates a CBT-based “Lifestyle Training” program adapted from addiction treatment to address internet addiction. Therapists reported that the approach was effective in helping patients reduce internet use, improve self-control, and rebuild daily routines. The treatment focuses on behavior change, structured routines, and replacing excessive internet use with meaningful real-life activities.

Core Ideas:
- Internet addiction can be treated using adapted CBT addiction programs
- Behavioral change is more effective than complete abstinence
- Addiction is linked to lack of structure, poor habits, and emotional triggers
- Real-life engagement is critical for recovery
- Internet addiction often co-occurs with other psychological issues

Practical Strategies:
- Track internet usage using a daily log or diary
- Establish a structured daily routine (sleep, work, activities)
- Replace excessive internet use with offline activities (socializing, hobbies)
- Increase real-life social interaction and responsibilities
- Use time blocks to control internet access
- Reduce unstructured free time that leads to overuse
- Focus on constructive and intentional use of the internet

Triggers:
- Lack of daily structure
- Excess free time
- Social isolation
- Emotional distress (anxiety, depression)
- Preference for online interaction over real-life interaction

Recovery Insights:
- Monitoring behavior (e.g., usage diary) is critical for awareness and change
- Building a structured daily routine significantly reduces addictive behavior
- Full abstinence is not realistic; controlled use is the goal
- Recovery improves when real-world engagement increases
